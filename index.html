Every game

Predicted score comes from the model: team ratings for scoring, play-by-play efficiency, returning production and roster talent, updated with every game played.
Hit % follows the model's side. For spreads and totals it's how often the model's side actually won at that size of edge (2019–2025); for moneylines it's the model's win chance from its predicted margin. A moneyline only counts as a good price if a market-blended check agrees, since the model alone is overconfident on long shots.
Return per $100 is the average result if you made this bet many times: hit % × payout, minus the stake. Moneylines use the best price found across sportsbooks; spreads and totals assume standard −110.
QB alerts flag teams whose usual starting quarterback missed their last game or has shared time. The model does not adjust for this yet, so check those games before betting.

Predicted score comes from team ratings for scoring and play-by-play efficiency (EPA), adjusted for opponents and weighted toward recent games, with last season carried into this one.
Win % converts the model's margin into a win chance (NFL games land about 13.5 points from the expected margin on average).
Hit % on props is the realistic chance, calibrated on 2024–25 real lines and confirmed on this season's: the raw model is overconfident. Strong = 56%+, Lean = 54–56%.
New model, still raw. Tested against about 4,800 real ESPN BET lines from 2024 and 2025. Props the model rated 70% or higher won about 65% in both seasons (break-even at normal prices is about 52–54%). The 65–70% group won 56–62%. Below 65% it didn't beat the line, so it makes no picks there.
Unlike the NBA and NFL, college books often miss: the model's biggest disagreements with the line did best. Still check injuries and depth charts, since the model can't see who's starting.

Projection = expected minutes × the player's per-minute production (recent games weighted more), adjusted for how much the opponent gives up to his position and for the pace of both teams. Players listed as out are removed.
Hit % blends the model with the sportsbook's own price. Tested against about 3,700 real prop lines, the model alone was overconfident (it leaned hard on unders that hit less often than it thought), while the market's price was accurate. The blend uses mostly the market plus the model's opinion. The "Check a line" box shows the model alone.
Return per $100 is hit % × payout, minus the stake. Best place compares DraftKings, Fliff and PrizePicks and picks the one that pays the most for the same bet. PrizePicks doesn't price single props: its odds shown are the standard pick'em equivalent (about −137, so 58% to break even), and picks must be combined into an entry.
